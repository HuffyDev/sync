Children and pets depend completely on adults in an emergency. A little planning makes it much easier to keep them safe, fed and calm, and to find each other if you are separated.

## Reunification: finding each other if separated

Families are often apart when disaster strikes, at work, school or daycare. Plan now how you will find each other.

- Choose an **out-of-area contact** that everyone can call or text. Long-distance calls sometimes work when local lines are busy.
- Agree on **meeting places**: one just outside your home, one in your neighbourhood and one outside your area. See the Communications guide.
- Learn the **emergency plans of your child's school or daycare**: where children will be taken and how they will be released. Make sure your contact details there are up to date, and list other adults allowed to pick up your child.
- Keep a **recent photo** of each child in your kit and on your phone, to help others find them.

### Teach children

- Their **full name**, your full name, address and a phone number, as soon as they are old enough.
- How to recognise helpers they can go to if lost, such as police officers, firefighters and teachers.
- To stay where they are and not wander off to look for you if separated in a crowd.

Put an **ID card** in each child's pocket or bag with their name, your name, phone numbers, allergies and medical needs.

## Kit items for babies and young children

Add these to your emergency kit. Check sizes and expiry dates every few months, as children grow quickly.

### Babies

- **Ready-to-feed infant formula**, which needs no water, plus bottles and nipples
- Baby food in pouches or jars, and a spoon
- Diapers, wipes and diaper rash cream
- A baby carrier or sling, which keeps your hands free
- Spare clothes, a warm hat and blankets
- Infant medicines, such as a fever reducer, with dosing instructions
- A thermometer

Breastfeeding remains the safest way to feed a baby in an emergency. If you breastfeed, keep going if you can.

### Children

- Snacks and drinks they like
- Spare clothes, sturdy shoes and rain gear
- Medicines, glasses and any medical equipment
- Comfort items, such as a favourite toy, blanket or book
- Games, crayons, paper and cards for quiet activities
- Copies of birth certificates, medical records and custody papers, if relevant

## Keeping children calm

Children watch adults closely. When you stay calm, they feel safer.

- **Reassure them** that you are there and adults are working to keep them safe.
- **Explain simply and honestly** what is happening, in words that suit their age.
- **Keep routines** for meals, play and bedtime as much as you can.
- **Limit what they see and hear** about the disaster on radio, TV or adult conversations.
- **Give them a role.** Small, safe jobs like handing out snacks or looking after a toy make them feel useful.
- **Encourage play.** Drawing, games and stories help children work through their feelings.
- Listen to their worries and accept their feelings. It is fine for them to be scared or sad.
- Expect some changes, such as clinginess, bedwetting or trouble sleeping. These usually ease with time.

See the Coping and Mental Health guide for more on supporting children.

## Keeping children safe from disaster hazards

Children are curious and may not understand new dangers. Watch them closely during and after an emergency.

- Keep children away from **generators, grills, camp stoves, candles and space heaters**.
- Keep them out of **floodwater**. It can be deep, fast and full of sewage and sharp objects.
- Keep them away from **downed power lines**, damaged buildings and debris.
- Store bleach, fuel, matches, lighters and medicines **out of reach**.
- Watch closely near pools, ponds and buckets of water. Young children can drown in small amounts of water.
- Give children plenty of safe drinking water, especially in heat. Never leave them in a parked car.
- Wash their hands often, especially before eating, as illness spreads easily in shelters and crowded homes.

> **Warning:** Carbon monoxide affects small children quickly. Never run fuel-burning equipment indoors.

## Kit items for pets

Prepare a kit for each pet, kept in an easy-to-carry container:

- **Food and water for at least 3 days**, and more if you can. Store food in an airtight, waterproof container.
- Bowls, and a manual can opener if needed
- **Medicines** and copies of medical and vaccination records in a waterproof bag
- A sturdy **leash, collar or harness with ID tags**
- A **crate or carrier** for each animal, large enough to stand and turn around in
- Litter and litter box, or waste bags
- A **recent photo of you with your pet**, to prove ownership if you are separated
- Familiar toys and bedding to reduce stress
- Feeding instructions, medical conditions and your vet's phone number, in case someone else must care for your pet

### Identification

Make sure every pet wears an **ID tag** with your phone number. If your pet is **microchipped**, keep the registration details up to date, and include an out-of-area contact.

## Evacuating with pets

- **If it is not safe for you to stay, it is not safe for your pets.** Take them with you whenever you can.
- **Leave early.** Waiting may mean you are forced to leave without your pets.
- Find out ahead of time which shelters, hotels and motels accept pets. Many public shelters only accept service animals, though some have pet-friendly areas.
- Ask friends or relatives outside your area if they could take your pets.
- Bring pets indoors at the first sign of danger, so you can find them quickly. Animals often hide when scared.
- Keep pets on a leash or in a carrier during the evacuation and in unfamiliar places.

### If you are not home

Arrange with a trusted neighbour to take your pets if a disaster happens while you are away. Make sure they have a key, know where the pet kit is and are comfortable with your animals.

A rescue alert sticker on your front door can tell rescuers how many pets are inside. If you leave with them, write "evacuated" on it if it is safe to do so.

## Pets after a disaster

- Familiar landmarks and smells may be gone, and pets can easily get lost. **Keep them on a leash or in a carrier** at first.
- Watch for downed power lines, debris, contaminated water and wildlife.
- Animals may act differently after a disaster, becoming aggressive, fearful or withdrawn. Watch them closely, especially around children.
- Give them fresh, safe water. Do not let them drink floodwater.
- Bring pets indoors in very hot or cold weather.
- If your pet is lost, contact local animal shelters and post notices with a photo. Check shelters in person if you can.
