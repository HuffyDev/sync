A good kit means you can look after yourself and your household when stores, power and phones are not available. Build it gradually. Something is always better than nothing.

## Grab-and-go bag: the essentials

Keep a bag for each person that you can pick up and carry if you have to leave quickly. Store it near the door or in the car.

- **Water:** 1 gallon (about 4 litres) per person per day, for at least **3 days**. If that is too heavy to carry, pack what you can, plus a way to treat water.
- **Food:** at least a 3-day supply of non-perishable food that needs no cooking, and a **manual can opener**.
- **Light:** a flashlight or headlamp for each person.
- **Radio:** battery-powered or hand-crank, ideally with the NOAA weather band in the US.
- **Extra batteries** for the flashlight and radio.
- **First aid kit** (see below).
- **Medications:** at least a **7-day supply** of prescription medicines, plus a written list of medicines and doses.
- **Whistle** to signal for help.
- **Phone charger, cable and a charged power bank.**
- **Cash** in small bills. Card machines and ATMs may not work without power.
- **Copies of important documents** in a waterproof bag (see below).
- **Local paper map**, with your meeting places marked.
- **Dust masks**, hand sanitizer and moist towelettes.

## First aid kit

A basic first aid kit should include:

- Adhesive bandages in several sizes
- Sterile gauze pads and rolls, and adhesive tape
- Elastic bandage
- Triangular bandage, which can be used as a sling
- Disposable gloves
- Antiseptic wipes and antibiotic ointment
- Tweezers and scissors
- Instant cold packs
- Pain and fever relievers suitable for each person
- Antihistamine for allergic reactions
- Oral rehydration salts
- Thermometer
- Emergency (space) blanket
- A small first aid guide or instruction card

Consider adding a commercial **tourniquet** and learning how to use it, especially if you live far from medical help.

Include any personal medical items: spare glasses, contact lens supplies, hearing aid batteries, an epinephrine auto-injector if prescribed, and glucose tablets for people with diabetes.

## Documents and money

Keep copies of important documents in a **waterproof bag or container**. You may need them to prove identity, get medical care, or apply for help.

- Identification: driver's licence, passport, birth certificates
- Insurance policies (home, health, car)
- Medical information: medicines, doses, allergies, conditions, doctors' contact details
- Bank account and credit card details
- Property deed, lease or proof of address
- Pet vaccination records and photos of pets
- Recent photos of family members, in case you are separated
- A written list of important phone numbers, including your out-of-area contact

A copy on a password-protected USB drive is useful, but keep paper copies too. Electronics may not work.

**Cash:** keep some cash in small bills and coins. During power outages, card payments and ATMs often do not work.

## Clothing, sanitation and tools

### Clothing and bedding

- A complete change of clothes for each person, suited to the season
- Sturdy, closed shoes
- Rain gear
- Warm hat, gloves and a warm layer
- Sleeping bag or warm blanket for each person

### Sanitation and hygiene

- Garbage bags and plastic ties
- Toilet paper
- Soap and hand sanitizer with at least 60% alcohol
- Moist towelettes
- Menstrual products
- Unscented household bleach and a medicine dropper (for disinfecting water and surfaces)
- A bucket with a lid for an emergency toilet, plus cat litter or sawdust

### Tools and supplies

- Wrench or pliers to turn off utilities
- Multi-tool or pocket knife
- Duct tape and plastic sheeting
- Matches in a waterproof container, or a lighter
- Fire extinguisher (for the home)
- Paper, pencil and permanent marker for notes
- Work gloves

## Supplies for babies, older adults and pets

Think about the specific needs of everyone in your household.

### Babies and young children

- Ready-to-feed infant formula (needs no water), bottles and nipples
- Baby food and a spoon
- Diapers, wipes and diaper rash cream
- Medicines for children, with dosing instructions
- A comfort item, small toys, books or games

### Older adults and people with disabilities

- Extra medicines and a written list of doses
- Spare glasses, hearing aid batteries and denture supplies
- Mobility aids, such as a spare cane
- Backup batteries or power for medical devices, and the supplier's phone number
- Copies of medical information and emergency contacts

### Pets

- Food and water for at least 3 days
- Bowls and a manual can opener if needed
- Leash, collar with ID tag, harness or carrier
- Medicines and vaccination records
- Litter and litter box, or waste bags
- A recent photo of your pet, in case you are separated

## The home kit: two weeks at home

During a long outage you may stay at home for days or weeks. Ready.gov suggests having supplies for **2 weeks** at home.

- **Water:** 1 gallon (about 4 litres) per person per day for 2 weeks. For a family of four, that is about 56 gallons (about 210 litres). Add more for hot weather, illness and pets.
- **Food:** a 2-week supply of non-perishable food your household will actually eat, such as canned meat, fish, beans, fruit and vegetables, peanut butter, crackers, cereal, dried fruit, nuts and shelf-stable milk.
- **Cooking:** a camp stove or grill with fuel, **for outdoor use only**.
- **Medicines:** talk to your doctor or pharmacist about keeping extra supplies of essential medicines.
- **Light and power:** flashlights and lanterns for each room you use, lots of spare batteries, and power banks.
- **Warmth:** extra blankets and sleeping bags.
- **Safety:** battery-powered **carbon monoxide alarm** and smoke alarm, and a fire extinguisher.
- **Water treatment:** unscented bleach, a dropper, disinfection tablets and a water filter.
- **Sanitation:** extra garbage bags, toilet paper, buckets and cleaning supplies.

## Keeping your kit ready

A kit is only useful if it works when you need it. Set a reminder to check it every 6 months, such as when the clocks change.

- **Water:** replace stored tap water every **6 months**. Keep commercially bottled water within its use-by date.
- **Food:** check use-by dates. Eat and replace food before it expires.
- **Batteries:** test flashlights and radios, and replace weak or old batteries. Store batteries outside devices to avoid leaks and corrosion.
- **Power banks:** recharge them every few months.
- **Medicines:** check expiry dates and replace them. Update the written medication list.
- **First aid kit:** replace used and expired items.
- **Clothing:** update clothes for the season and as children grow.
- **Documents:** update copies and phone numbers when things change.

Store kits in a cool, dry place. Keep a smaller kit in the car and at work. Make sure everyone in the household knows where the kits are.
