Earthquakes strike without warning, and the first seconds matter most. This guide covers what to do during the shaking, in the minutes after, and if you are near the coast.

## Drop, Cover, Hold On

When the ground starts shaking:

1. **Drop** onto your hands and knees wherever you are. This stops you being knocked over and lets you crawl if needed.
2. **Cover** your head and neck with one arm and hand. If a sturdy table or desk is nearby, crawl under it. If not, crawl next to an interior wall, away from windows. Stay on your knees, bent over to protect your vital organs.
3. **Hold On** until the shaking stops. Under a table, hold on to it with one hand and be ready to move with it if it shifts.

### What not to do

- **Do not run outside** during shaking. Most injuries happen when people try to move and are hit by falling objects or glass.
- **Do not stand in a doorway.** In modern homes doorways are no stronger than other parts of the house, and they do not protect you from falling objects.
- Stay away from windows, glass, outside walls and anything that could fall, such as tall furniture.

> **Warning:** Drop, Cover and Hold On every time the ground shakes, including during aftershocks.

## If you are in bed, outdoors or driving

- **In bed:** Stay there. Lie face down and cover your head and neck with a pillow.
- **Using a wheelchair or walker:** Lock the wheels. Bend over and cover your head and neck with your arms or a book.
- **Outdoors:** Stay outside. Move away from buildings, streetlights, power lines and trees if you can do so safely, then Drop, Cover and Hold On.
- **Driving:** Pull over to a clear place and stop. Set the parking brake. Avoid bridges, overpasses and power lines. Stay inside the vehicle with your seat belt on until the shaking stops.
- **In a crowded public place:** Do not rush for the exits. Drop, Cover and Hold On where you are.
- **Near the coast:** When the shaking stops, move to high ground right away. See the tsunami section below.

## Right after the shaking stops

1. Check yourself for injuries, then help others. See the First Aid guide.
2. Put on sturdy shoes. There may be broken glass and debris everywhere.
3. If your building is damaged, get out carefully and go to an open area away from buildings.
4. Check for fires, gas leaks and damaged electrical wiring.
5. Use flashlights, not candles, matches or lighters, in case gas is leaking.
6. Text rather than call, to keep phone lines free for emergencies.
7. Listen to a battery or hand-crank radio for information.

### If you are trapped

- Do not light a match or lighter.
- Cover your mouth with a cloth or your clothing to keep out dust.
- Tap on a pipe or wall, or use a whistle, so rescuers can hear you. Shout only as a last resort, as shouting can make you breathe in dust.
- Move as little as possible to avoid stirring up dust.

## Aftershocks

Aftershocks are smaller earthquakes that follow the main one. They can happen in the minutes, days, weeks and even months afterwards, and some are strong enough to cause more damage.

- **Drop, Cover and Hold On** every time you feel shaking.
- Stay out of damaged buildings. An aftershock can bring down a weakened structure.
- Be careful opening cupboards and closets. Contents may have shifted and can fall.
- Stay away from damaged chimneys, walls and slopes that might collapse or slide.
- Keep sturdy shoes, a flashlight and a whistle next to your bed in case of night-time aftershocks.

Very occasionally, an earthquake is followed by a larger one. Keep following official advice, and do not return to damaged buildings until they have been inspected.

## Gas leaks and utilities

Earthquakes can break gas lines, water pipes and wiring.

### If you smell gas

If you smell gas or hear a blowing or hissing sound:

1. **Open a window if you can, and leave the building immediately.**
2. Do not use light switches, phones, lighters, matches or anything that could make a spark.
3. If you know how and it is safe, turn off the gas at the outside main valve.
4. Report the leak to the gas company or emergency services from outside, well away from the building.

Only turn off the gas if you smell it, hear it, or officials tell you to. Once it is off, **only a professional should turn it back on**. See the Home Security and Safety guide.

### Electricity and water

- If you see sparks, broken wires or smell hot insulation, turn off the power at the main breaker if you can do so safely.
- If water pipes are broken, shut off the main water valve.
- Do not drink tap water until officials say it is safe. See the Water guide.

## Tsunami warning signs near the coast

A tsunami can follow an earthquake. Near the coast, **natural warnings may be the only warning you get**. If you notice any of these, move to high ground or inland immediately:

- **Strong shaking** that makes it hard to stand, or a **long earthquake** that lasts 20 seconds or more
- The ocean suddenly **drawing back** and exposing the sea floor
- A **loud roar** from the ocean, like a train or jet
- Unusual water rising quickly along the shore

### What to do

- **Go on foot if you can.** Roads may be blocked or damaged.
- Go as high and as far inland as possible. If you cannot get out of the area, go to an upper floor or roof of a tall, sturdy building as a last resort.
- Do not go to the shore to watch.
- Tsunamis come as a series of waves that can continue for hours. The first wave is often not the largest.
- Stay away from the coast until officials say it is safe.

> **Warning:** Do not wait for an official warning if you see natural signs. Get to high ground now.

## Preparing your home

Most earthquake injuries come from falling objects, not collapsing buildings. Reduce the danger now:

- Secure tall, heavy furniture such as bookcases and dressers to the wall.
- Strap the water heater to wall studs.
- Store heavy and breakable items on low shelves.
- Hang heavy pictures and mirrors away from beds and seats.
- Know where and how to shut off gas, water and electricity.
- Practise Drop, Cover and Hold On with everyone in your home, including children.
- Keep an emergency kit, sturdy shoes and a flashlight where you can reach them quickly.
- Pick safe spots in each room, such as under a sturdy table.
