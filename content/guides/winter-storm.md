Winter storms can bring heavy snow, freezing rain, ice and extreme cold, often with long power outages. This guide covers what to do before, during and after the storm. For staying warm without power, see the Shelter and Warmth guide.

## During the storm: the most important rules

- **Stay indoors** and off the roads unless you truly must travel.
- Keep everyone in one or two rooms to save heat. Close off unused rooms.
- Wear layers of loose, warm clothing, a hat and dry socks. Use blankets and sleeping bags.
- **Never** use a generator, grill, camp stove or charcoal indoors, or in a garage, even with the door open.
- Keep generators **at least 20 feet (6 metres)** from windows, doors and vents.
- Eat regular meals and drink fluids. Food gives the body energy to make heat. Avoid alcohol, which makes you lose body heat faster.
- Check on older neighbours, people living alone and anyone with a medical condition, if it is safe to do so.
- Watch everyone for signs of hypothermia and frostbite.

> **Warning:** Hypothermia and frostbite can happen indoors in an unheated home. Check on babies and older adults often.

## Carbon monoxide and blocked vents

Carbon monoxide (CO) is a poisonous gas you cannot see or smell. Snow and ice can block the vents and chimneys that carry it out of your home.

- After heavy snow, **clear snow away from outside vents** for the furnace, water heater, clothes dryer and any fuel-burning appliance. Check again after drifting or more snowfall.
- Keep chimneys and flues clear. Do not use a fireplace if the chimney may be blocked by snow or ice.
- **Clear snow from a car's exhaust pipe** before starting the engine. Never run a car in a closed garage.
- Make sure CO alarms have working batteries.

Common symptoms of CO poisoning are headache, dizziness, weakness, upset stomach or vomiting, chest pain and confusion. Several people feeling sick at the same time is a warning sign.

If a CO alarm sounds or anyone has symptoms, **get everyone into fresh air immediately** and call for help from outside. Do not go back in until the home has been checked. See the First Aid guide.

## Before the storm

When a winter storm watch or warning is issued, use the time to prepare.

- A **winter storm watch** means a storm is possible. A **winter storm warning** means it is happening or expected soon.
- Gather water, food that needs no cooking, medicines, flashlights, batteries and a battery or hand-crank radio.
- Charge phones and power banks.
- Fill your vehicle's fuel tank. Keep a winter kit in the car.
- Have extra blankets, warm clothing and hats for everyone.
- Bring pets indoors.
- Know how to protect pipes from freezing. The Shelter and Warmth guide explains how.
- Have rock salt or sand for walkways, and a snow shovel.
- Test smoke and CO alarms.

## Ice, snow shovelling and falls

Falls on ice and heart strain from shovelling cause many injuries in winter storms.

- Walk slowly with short steps on icy ground. Wear boots with good grip.
- Spread rock salt, sand or cat litter on steps and paths.
- Watch for ice falling from roofs, trees and power lines.

### Shovelling safely

Shovelling heavy snow is hard work and can bring on a heart attack, especially in people who are older or have heart disease.

- Take frequent breaks. Push snow rather than lifting it where you can.
- Lift small amounts with your legs, not your back.
- Stop and get help if you feel chest pain, shortness of breath, dizziness or pain spreading to the arm, neck or jaw.
- Dress in layers, cover your head and hands, and change out of wet clothing.

> **Warning:** Stay well away from downed or sagging power lines, especially lines weighed down by ice. Assume they are live.

## Snow and ice on the roof

Heavy, wet snow and ice can overload a roof. Watch for these warning signs:

- Sagging ceilings or roof lines
- Creaking, cracking or popping sounds
- New cracks in walls or ceilings
- Doors or windows that suddenly stick
- Leaks or water stains

If you see these signs, leave that part of the building and seek advice from a professional.

To remove snow, use a **long-handled roof rake from the ground**. Work slowly and keep away from the area where snow will fall. Do not climb onto an icy roof. Keep ladders and rakes well away from power lines.

Icicles and ice dams along the roof edge can fall and injure people. Keep children and pets away from the area under them.

## Driving and being stranded in a vehicle

Avoid driving during a winter storm. If you must travel, tell someone your route and expected arrival time, and carry a winter car kit: blankets, warm clothes, water, snacks, a flashlight, a small shovel, sand or cat litter for traction, a scraper and a phone charger.

### If you are stranded

1. **Stay in the vehicle.** It is easier for rescuers to find you, and you are protected from the wind. Leave only if you can see a building close by where you can safely shelter.
2. Tie a bright cloth to the antenna or door, or raise the hood when it stops snowing, to signal for help.
3. **Clear snow from the exhaust pipe** to prevent carbon monoxide poisoning.
4. Run the engine and heater about **10 minutes each hour**. Open a downwind window slightly for fresh air while it runs.
5. Turn on the inside light while the engine runs so you can be seen.
6. Move your arms and legs to keep blood flowing. Huddle together and use floor mats, newspapers or clothing for warmth.
7. Drink fluids to avoid dehydration.

## After the storm

- Keep listening to local radio for updates and warnings.
- Stay off roads until they are cleared.
- Check on neighbours, especially older adults and people living alone.
- Keep clearing vents and the car exhaust pipe after any new snowfall or drifting.

### Frozen pipes

If a tap only trickles, a pipe may be frozen. Keep the tap open and apply gentle heat to the frozen section with a hair dryer, an electric heating pad or towels soaked in hot water.

> **Warning:** Never thaw pipes with a blowtorch, propane or kerosene heater, or any open flame. Call a plumber if you cannot find or reach the frozen section.

### Watch for frostbite and hypothermia

Frostbite causes numbness and skin that looks white or greyish-yellow and feels firm or waxy. Signs of hypothermia include shivering, exhaustion, confusion, fumbling hands, memory loss, slurred speech and drowsiness. Babies may have bright red, cold skin and very low energy. See the First Aid guide for what to do.
