Tornadoes can form quickly and destroy buildings in seconds. Knowing ahead of time exactly where you will shelter is the most important thing you can do.

## Watch or warning: what they mean

- A **tornado watch** means conditions are right for tornadoes in and near the area. **Get ready.** Review your plan, check your safe place and keep a radio on.
- A **tornado warning** means a tornado has been seen or shown on weather radar. **Take shelter immediately.** Do not wait to see it.

Listen to NOAA Weather Radio, a battery or hand-crank radio, or local outdoor sirens. Sirens are designed to warn people outdoors and may not be heard inside.

### Natural danger signs

- A dark, often greenish sky
- Large hail
- A large, dark, low-lying cloud, especially if it is rotating
- A loud roar, similar to a freight train

Tornadoes can be hidden by heavy rain or darkness. If you hear a roar or see debris in the air, take shelter at once.

> **Warning:** When a tornado warning is issued, go to your safe place immediately. You may have only minutes.

## Where to shelter in a house

The safest places, from best to good:

1. A **safe room** built to FEMA guidelines or a certified **storm shelter**
2. A **basement**
3. A small, **windowless interior room on the lowest floor**, such as a closet, bathroom or inner hallway

Put as many walls as possible between you and the outside.

### Protect yourself

- Get under something sturdy, such as a heavy table or workbench, if you can.
- **Cover your head and neck** with your arms.
- Cover your body with a mattress, sleeping bag or thick blankets.
- Wear sturdy shoes, and a helmet if you have one, such as a bicycle or sports helmet. Head injuries cause many tornado deaths.
- Stay away from windows. **Do not open windows.** It wastes time and does not protect your home.
- In a basement, avoid spots directly under heavy objects on the floor above, such as a piano or fridge.

## Mobile homes are not safe

Mobile and manufactured homes can be overturned or destroyed by tornadoes, **even if they are tied down**.

- Plan ahead. Know where the nearest sturdy building or storm shelter is, and how long it takes to get there.
- **Leave as soon as a tornado watch is issued**, or when severe weather is expected, and go to your planned shelter. Waiting for a warning may not give you enough time.
- Check whether your community or park has a designated storm shelter.

If you cannot reach a building, a ditch or low area away from vehicles and trees may be a last resort. Lie flat and cover your head and neck with your arms. Watch for flooding in low areas.

> **Warning:** Do not stay in a mobile home during a tornado warning.

## In a vehicle or outdoors

There is no fully safe option in a vehicle or outdoors. Try to get to a sturdy building or shelter before the storm arrives.

### If you are driving

- If the tornado is far away and traffic allows, you may be able to drive out of its path at a right angle to its direction of travel.
- If you are caught by flying debris or extreme wind, **pull over and park** out of traffic lanes.
- **Option 1:** Stay in the vehicle with your seat belt on. Put your head down below the windows and cover it with your hands and a coat or blanket.
- **Option 2:** If you can get noticeably lower than the road, leave the vehicle, lie flat in that low area and cover your head and neck with your arms.

### Never shelter under a bridge or overpass

The space under an overpass can speed up wind and flying debris. It is more dangerous than an open low spot.

## In large buildings

Schools, shops, offices, hospitals and places of worship should have tornado plans. Follow staff instructions.

- Go to the designated shelter area, usually an interior hallway or room on the lowest floor.
- Stay away from **large open rooms** with wide roofs, such as gyms, auditoriums, cafeterias and large shop floors. Their roofs can collapse.
- Stay away from windows, glass doors and atriums.
- In a tall building, go to a small interior room or stairwell on the lowest floor possible. Do not use lifts or elevators, as power may fail.
- Crouch down and cover your head and neck with your arms.

## After a tornado

- Stay in your shelter until the danger has passed. More storms may follow.
- Check yourself and others for injuries. See the First Aid guide.
- Wear sturdy shoes, long sleeves and work gloves. Broken glass and nails are everywhere.
- **Stay away from downed power lines** and report them. Assume they are live.
- Stay out of damaged buildings until they have been checked.
- If you smell gas or hear hissing, leave right away and report it from outside.
- Use flashlights, not candles or matches.
- Text rather than call so lines stay free for emergencies.
- Take photos of damage for insurance.

> **Warning:** Never run a generator indoors or in a garage. Keep it at least 20 feet (6 metres) from windows, doors and vents.

## Making a tornado plan

- Choose your safe place at home, at work and at school before you need it.
- Practise getting there quickly with everyone in your home, including children and older adults.
- Keep shoes, a flashlight, a whistle and helmets in or near your safe place.
- Keep a radio with spare batteries for weather alerts.
- If you have pets, plan how to bring them with you. Practise getting them into carriers.
- Agree on a meeting place and an out-of-area contact in case you are separated. See the Communications guide.
- Help neighbours who may need assistance, such as older adults or people with disabilities, make their own plans.
