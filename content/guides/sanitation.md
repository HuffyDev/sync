When water or sewer service fails, germs from human waste can spread quickly and cause diarrhoea and other illness. Good sanitation and handwashing are some of the most effective ways to keep everyone healthy.

## When the toilet will not flush

First find out which problem you have.

**If water is not running, but the sewer is working:**

- You can flush by pouring a bucket of water (about 1 to 2 gallons, or 4 to 8 litres) quickly into the toilet bowl. The force of the water pushes waste through.
- Use non-drinking water for this, such as pool water, rainwater or used washing water.
- To save water, flush only when needed.

**If the sewer is backed up or damaged**, or officials tell you not to flush:

- **Do not flush.** Water and waste may back up into your home or a neighbour's.
- Do not pour water down drains.
- Use an emergency bucket toilet instead (see the next section).

**Signs of a sewer problem:** toilets or drains gurgling or backing up, sewage coming up in a bathtub or floor drain, or official warnings.

If your home uses an electric pump for sewage (some basements, condos and septic systems), the system may stop when the power is out. Limit use until power returns.

## Making an emergency bucket toilet

You can make a simple toilet from a sturdy bucket with a lid, or by lining the existing toilet bowl.

**You need:**

- A sturdy bucket (about 5 gallons / 20 litres) with a tight lid, or your existing toilet with the water removed from the bowl
- Heavy-duty plastic garbage bags
- Something absorbent: cat litter, sawdust, wood shavings, shredded paper or dirt
- Toilet paper, hand sanitizer and a separate sealed container for full bags

**How to set it up:**

1. Line the bucket or toilet bowl with **two** plastic bags (double bag).
2. Put a layer of cat litter, sawdust or other absorbent material in the bottom.
3. After each use, add another scoop of absorbent material to cover the waste. This reduces smell and flies.
4. Keep the lid on between uses.
5. When the bag is about half full, tie the inner bag closed, then tie the outer bag.

**Two-bucket option:** Some people use one bucket for urine and one for faeces. Keeping urine separate makes the solid waste bucket smell less and fill more slowly.

Keep the toilet in a private, well-ventilated place, away from where you prepare food.

## Storing and getting rid of waste

Full waste bags carry germs. Handle them carefully.

- Wear gloves if you have them. Wash your hands afterwards.
- Put tied bags into a larger **container with a tight lid**, such as a trash can.
- Keep it away from living areas, food and water supplies, and out of reach of children and pets.
- Keep it in shade if you can to reduce smell.
- Listen for instructions from local officials about how and when waste will be collected.

**If you must bury waste:**

- Dig a hole at least 6 to 8 inches (15 to 20 cm) deep.
- Choose a spot at least **200 feet (about 60 metres) from any well, stream, lake or other water source**, and away from gardens.
- Cover it completely with soil.

Never dump human waste into storm drains, streams or lakes. It can contaminate drinking water for you and others.

## Handwashing without running water

Washing hands with soap is one of the best ways to prevent illness. Always wash after using the toilet, changing diapers, handling waste or trash, and before preparing food, eating or feeding a child.

**Make a simple handwashing station:**

- Fill a jug, bottle or container with a tap or spout with clean water.
- A **tippy tap** is a jug hung so a foot pedal or string tips it to pour a small stream of water, so you do not touch it with dirty hands.
- Set a bucket or basin underneath to catch the used water.
- Keep soap nearby.

**How to wash:**

1. Wet your hands with clean water.
2. Lather with soap, covering the backs of your hands, between your fingers and under your nails.
3. Scrub for at least **20 seconds**.
4. Rinse with clean water.
5. Dry with a clean towel or air dry.

Used handwashing water can be used to flush toilets.

## Hand sanitizer

When you cannot wash with soap and water, use a hand sanitizer that contains **at least 60% alcohol**.

- Put enough on to cover all surfaces of both hands.
- Rub your hands together until they feel dry. This takes about 20 seconds. Do not wipe it off.
- Sanitizer does **not** work well when hands are visibly dirty or greasy. Wipe off dirt first, or wash with soap and water when you can.
- It does not remove all types of germs or chemicals.
- Keep sanitizer away from flames and out of reach of young children. Swallowing it can cause alcohol poisoning.

Keep sanitizer next to the emergency toilet and where food is prepared.

## Disinfecting surfaces with bleach

Clean first, then disinfect. Dirt protects germs.

**CDC recipe for a bleach disinfecting solution:**

- **5 tablespoons (1/3 cup, about 80 mL)** of unscented household bleach per **gallon (about 4 litres)** of room-temperature water, or
- **4 teaspoons** of unscented bleach per **quart (about 1 litre)** of room-temperature water.

**How to use it:**

1. Wear gloves if you have them.
2. Wash the surface with soap and water.
3. Apply the bleach solution and leave it wet for at least **1 minute**.
4. Make a fresh batch each day. It loses strength over time.
5. Work with good ventilation.

Use it on toilet areas, door handles, counters and anything soiled with waste or vomit.

> **Warning:** Never mix bleach with ammonia or any other cleaner. Mixing can release toxic gases.

Check the bleach label: some products have a different strength, and some are labelled as not for disinfection. Keep bleach away from children.

## Trash and food waste

Trash attracts flies, rats and other pests that can spread disease.

- Put food scraps and trash in **sealed plastic bags** inside containers with tight lids.
- Keep trash away from living areas, and out of reach of children, pets and wildlife.
- Rinse cans and containers with used water before throwing them away to reduce smell.
- Keep spoiled food from the fridge and freezer bagged and sealed until it can be collected.
- Keep human waste bags separate from regular trash, and follow local instructions for both.
- Never burn plastic or trash indoors. Burning trash outdoors may also be banned, and produces harmful smoke.

Wash your hands after handling trash.

## Menstrual and baby hygiene

### Menstrual hygiene

- Keep a supply of pads, tampons or reusable products in your emergency kit.
- Change products regularly. Wash hands before and after.
- Wrap used products and put them in a sealed bag with other trash. Do not flush them, especially if the sewer may be damaged.
- Reusable products should be washed with soap and clean water and dried thoroughly, in sunlight if possible.

### Babies and young children

- Keep a supply of **disposable diapers** and baby wipes.
- Change diapers away from food areas, on a surface you can clean.
- Seal used diapers in plastic bags and store them with other waste in a container with a tight lid.
- Wash your hands, and the baby's hands, after every change.
- Wash bottles and feeding equipment with bottled, boiled or disinfected water.
- Give sponge baths with clean water, and make sure young children do not swallow bath water if the water supply is in doubt.

## Watching for illness spreading

Poor sanitation can spread illnesses that cause **diarrhoea and vomiting**. Act early.

**Watch for:**

- Diarrhoea or vomiting in more than one person
- Fever
- Signs of dehydration: thirst, dry mouth, little or dark urine, dizziness, and in babies fewer wet diapers or no tears

**If someone is ill:**

- Keep them away from food preparation.
- If you can, give them a separate toilet or bucket. Disinfect toilet areas often with bleach solution.
- Everyone should wash hands with soap, especially after helping a sick person.
- Do not share towels, cups or utensils.
- Give plenty of safe fluids. Use oral rehydration solution if available. The First Aid guide has a home recipe.

Seek medical help urgently for bloody diarrhoea, high fever, confusion, inability to keep fluids down, or signs of dehydration in babies, young children and older adults.
