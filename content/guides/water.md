You can survive much longer without food than without water. This guide explains how much you need, where to find it, and how to make doubtful water safer to drink.

## How much water you need

Plan for **1 gallon (about 4 litres) per person per day**. This covers drinking plus a small amount for food preparation and basic hygiene.

- Store at least a **3-day supply** for each person.
- If you can, store a **2-week supply** for home use.
- Store extra for children, nursing mothers, people who are ill, and anyone in hot weather, where needs can double.
- Remember pets. They need water too.

If water is short, do not ration drinking water to the point of thirst. Drink what you need today and look for more tomorrow. Cut back on activity and stay out of the heat to reduce how much you need. Use the least clean water for flushing and cleaning, and save the best water for drinking, cooking and infant formula.

## Hidden water in your home

Your home holds more safe water than you may think.

- **Water heater tank:** Turn off the power or gas first and let the water cool. Open a hot water tap somewhere in the house to let air in, then drain water from the valve at the bottom of the tank. Some sediment at first is normal. Do not turn the heater back on until the tank is full again.
- **Ice cubes** and ice in the freezer can be melted and used.
- **Water pipes:** If officials say the water supply may be contaminated, shut off the main water valve to keep bad water out. Then open the highest faucet in the house to let air in, and collect water from the lowest faucet.
- **Toilet tank (not the bowl):** Water in the tank behind the toilet can be used only if no chemical cleaners or colour tablets have been added. Disinfect it before drinking.
- Canned fruits and vegetables also contain liquid you can drink.

> **Warning:** Never drink water from the toilet bowl.

## Water you should NOT drink

Some water cannot be made safe at home, because boiling and bleach do not remove chemicals or fuel.

- Swimming pool or spa water (use it only for flushing toilets or cleaning)
- Waterbed water (it often contains chemicals)
- Water from car radiators or boiler heating systems
- Floodwater
- Any water that smells of fuel or chemicals, or has an oily sheen

If you must use surface water from a stream, lake or rain, collect it from the cleanest source you can find and both filter and disinfect it before drinking.

## Clarify cloudy water first

Dirt and particles protect germs from bleach and other disinfectants. Clear the water before disinfecting it.

1. Let the water stand in a container until the sediment settles to the bottom.
2. Carefully pour or scoop the clearer water off the top into a clean container.
3. Or filter it through a clean cloth, paper towel or coffee filter.
4. Then disinfect it by boiling or with bleach.

Clarifying alone does not make water safe to drink. It is only the first step.

## Boiling: the most reliable method

Boiling kills disease-causing germs, including viruses, bacteria and parasites.

1. If the water is cloudy, settle and filter it first.
2. Bring the water to a **rolling boil for 1 minute**.
3. At elevations above **6,500 feet (about 2,000 metres)**, boil for **3 minutes**.
4. Let it cool naturally. Store it in clean, covered containers.

To improve the flat taste, pour the cooled water back and forth between two clean containers, or add a pinch of salt to each litre or quart.

Boiling does not remove chemicals, fuel or heavy metals. If the water may be contaminated with chemicals, find another source.

> **Warning:** Never boil water or cook with a camp stove, grill or other fuel-burning device indoors. Use it outside, away from windows and doors.

## Disinfecting with household bleach

If you cannot boil water, you can disinfect it with plain household bleach, as advised by the US EPA.

- Use **unscented** liquid chlorine bleach that contains **6% or 8.25% sodium hypochlorite**. Check the label.
- Do not use scented, colour-safe or "splash-less" bleach, or bleach with added cleaners.
- Use bleach that has been stored at room temperature and is less than about a year old.

How much to add to clear water:

- **6% bleach:** 2 drops per litre or quart, **8 drops per gallon**.
- **8.25% bleach:** 2 drops per litre or quart, **6 drops per gallon**.
- If the water is **cloudy, coloured or very cold**, use **double** the amount.

Then:

1. Stir the water well.
2. Wait at least **30 minutes**.
3. The water should have a slight chlorine smell.
4. If it does not, repeat the dose and wait another **15 minutes** before using it.

Use a clean dropper. If you have no dropper, a clean spoon and a clean cloth strip can help you release drops one at a time. If the chlorine taste is strong, let the water stand open to the air for a few hours.

## Tablets and portable filters

**Disinfection tablets** (chlorine, chlorine dioxide or iodine) are made for treating drinking water. Follow the instructions on the package exactly, including the waiting time.

- Chlorine dioxide is more effective against the parasite *Cryptosporidium* than chlorine or iodine, but may need a longer wait.
- Iodine is not recommended for pregnant women, people with thyroid problems or people with a known iodine allergy, and is not meant for long-term use.

**Portable water filters** remove many parasites and some bacteria, but most do **not** remove viruses.

- Choose a filter with a pore size of 1 micron or smaller to remove parasites such as *Giardia* and *Cryptosporidium*.
- After filtering, **also disinfect** the water with boiling, bleach or tablets to deal with viruses.
- Filters do not remove chemicals unless the manufacturer specifically says so.

## Storing water safely

Good containers keep treated water safe for longer.

- Use food-grade containers with tight lids, such as unopened commercial bottled water or clean drink bottles.
- Do not use containers that held milk or fruit juice, since they are hard to clean fully, or anything that held chemicals.
- Clean containers with soap and water and rinse them. To sanitize, CDC suggests swishing a solution of 1 teaspoon unscented bleach in 1 quart (about 1 litre) of water so it touches all inside surfaces, then rinsing.
- Label containers with the date. Store them somewhere cool and dark, away from fuel, pesticides and other chemicals.
- Replace stored tap water every **6 months**. Keep commercial bottled water within its use-by date.
- Pour water out rather than dipping cups or hands into the container.

## Boil-water advisories and infant formula

If local officials issue a boil-water advisory, their instructions come first. Follow them until they say the water is safe again.

During an advisory, use bottled, boiled or disinfected water for:

- Drinking and making drinks and ice
- Cooking, and washing fruit and vegetables
- Brushing teeth
- Preparing infant formula

You can usually still bathe and shower, but do not swallow the water. Supervise children and give babies sponge baths.

### Infant formula

- **Ready-to-feed** liquid formula needs no water and is the safest choice when water is in doubt.
- For powdered or concentrated formula, use bottled water if you have it. If not, use water that has been boiled for 1 minute (3 minutes at high altitude) and allowed to cool.
- Breastfeeding remains the safest way to feed a baby during emergencies. Keep breastfeeding if you can.
- Clean bottles and nipples with bottled, boiled or disinfected water.
