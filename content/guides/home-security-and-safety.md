After a disaster, damaged utilities, empty homes and dishonest people can all put you at risk. This guide covers how to control your utilities safely, secure your home, protect yourself from scams and keep important documents safe.

## Gas: shut it off only if needed

If you **smell gas**, hear a **blowing or hissing sound**, or see a broken gas line:

1. **Leave the building immediately.** Get everyone out.
2. Do not use light switches, phones, lighters, matches or anything that could make a spark inside.
3. If you can do so safely, turn off the gas at the **main valve outside**.
4. Call the gas company or emergency services **from outside**, well away from the building.

Only turn off the gas if you smell gas, hear a leak, or officials tell you to. Do not shut it off as a precaution.

### How the gas meter valve works

The main shut-off valve is usually on the pipe next to the meter. Use a wrench to give it a **quarter turn**, so the valve sits **crosswise to the pipe**. Keep a wrench near the meter, and learn where the valve is before you need it.

> **Warning:** Once the gas is turned off, **only a professional should turn it back on**. Never try to restore it yourself. This may take days or weeks after a major disaster.

## Electricity and water shut-offs

Learn where your **main electrical panel** (breaker box or fuse box) and **main water valve** are now, and show other adults in the home.

### Electricity

Turn off power at the main breaker if:

- You see sparks or broken or frayed wires
- You smell hot insulation
- Water is rising toward outlets or the panel
- Officials tell you to

**Only touch the panel if you are dry and not standing in water.** If the panel is wet or in a flooded area, stay away and call an electrician or the utility.

To protect the main breaker from a surge, turn off individual circuit breakers first, then the main one.

### Water

Shut off the main water valve if pipes are broken or leaking, or if officials say the water supply may be contaminated. This also keeps clean water already in your pipes and water heater from being mixed with bad water. Label the valve and keep any tool you need next to it.

## Securing your home

- **Lock doors and windows**, including when you are home.
- Cover broken windows and doors with plywood or heavy plastic, and cover roof damage with a tarp, if it is safe to do so. Do not climb onto damaged roofs.
- Use battery-powered or solar lights outside at night.
- Keep valuables, cash, medicines and documents out of sight.
- Do not post on social media or leave obvious signs that your home is empty.
- If you evacuate, take your important documents and valuables with you.
- Get to know your neighbours. Agree to watch each other's homes and check in regularly.

### Personal safety

- Do not confront anyone who is stealing or behaving threateningly. Your safety matters more than property. Report it to police.
- Move around in pairs or groups when possible, and tell someone where you are going.
- Ask to see identification from anyone who comes to your door claiming to be from a utility, government agency or aid group.

## Recognising scams after a disaster

Scammers target people after disasters, when they are stressed and need help quickly.

### Common scams

- **Fake officials:** people claiming to be from FEMA or other agencies who ask for money or bank details. FEMA does not charge to apply for help, and inspectors never charge for inspections. Real staff carry official photo ID.
- **Fake contractors:** people who show up offering fast repairs, then take a deposit and disappear, or do poor work.
- **Fake charities:** requests for donations, often by phone, text or social media, that sound urgent.
- **Price gouging:** sharply higher prices for essentials such as water, fuel and generators.

### Protect yourself

- **Never give your Social Security number, bank or card details** to someone who contacts you unexpectedly.
- Do not pay anyone to "speed up" a claim or aid application.
- **Do not pay in cash**, and do not pay the full cost of repairs up front.
- Report suspected scams to local police, your state attorney general or the FTC.

## Hiring contractors safely

Many dishonest contractors go to disaster areas looking for work. Before you hire anyone:

- Get **written estimates** from more than one contractor.
- Ask for proof of **licence and insurance**, and check with your local or state licensing office if you can.
- Ask for references from local customers, and call them.
- Get a **written contract** that lists the work, materials, cost and timeline. Read it fully before you sign, and never sign a blank or partly blank contract.
- Do not pay the full amount up front. Pay by cheque or card, not cash, and make the final payment only when the work is done and you are satisfied.
- Be wary of anyone who knocks on your door uninvited, pressures you to decide quickly, or asks you to get permits yourself.

Contact your insurance company before agreeing to major repairs, so you know what is covered.

## Protecting important documents

Replacing lost documents after a disaster can take a long time. Gather them now.

### What to keep

- Identity documents: driver's licences, passports, birth and marriage certificates, Social Security cards
- Insurance policies for home, car, health and life
- Deeds, mortgage papers, leases and vehicle titles
- Medical records, prescriptions and a medication list
- Bank and credit card account numbers, with contact numbers
- Wills and powers of attorney
- Pet records and photos
- A list of emergency contacts

### How to keep them

- Store originals in a **waterproof and fire-resistant container**.
- Keep **copies** in your grab-and-go bag in a waterproof bag.
- Keep a digital copy on a password-protected or encrypted USB drive, or with a trusted person outside your area.
- Keep some **cash in small bills**, as cards and ATMs may not work.

## Documenting damage for insurance and aid

Good records make insurance claims and aid applications much easier.

- **Before a disaster**, take photos or video of every room, including the contents of cupboards and closets. Note the make, model and serial number of valuable items.
- **After a disaster**, take photos or video of all damage **before you start cleaning up or making repairs**, as long as it is safe.
- Make temporary repairs to prevent more damage, such as covering a roof with a tarp, and keep receipts for materials.
- Keep receipts for all disaster-related costs, such as hotel stays, food and clean-up supplies.
- Contact your insurance company as soon as you can. Write down the date, time and name of everyone you talk to.
- Do not throw away damaged items until an adjuster has seen them or told you that you can, unless they are a health hazard, such as spoiled food. Photograph them first.
